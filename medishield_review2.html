01 — THE CORE PROBLEM

A confident prediction is not the same thing as a trustworthy one.

A conventional pipeline stops the moment it produces a number. MediShield treats that number as the beginning of the question, not the end of it — checking whether the model would still say the same thing under pressure before it lets the prediction through.

FAILURE MODES

A high-confidence prediction can still be wrong. Models can become unstable after small input changes, react badly to adversarial perturbations, flip their prediction after routine image degradation — noise, blur, compression, brightness shifts — produce explanations that shift substantially under perturbation, or simply encounter an input that falls outside the distribution they learned from. None of these failure modes announce themselves through the confidence score alone.

04 — DATASET

HAM10000, narrowed to three classes on purpose.

10,015 dermoscopic images across seven diagnostic categories. MediShield restricts itself to three, then works from an approximately balanced subset rather than the raw, heavily skewed distribution.

WORKING SUBSET

Target: roughly 600 images per class, ~1,800 images total, split 70 / 15 / 15 into training, validation, and test — a target rather than a hard requirement, finalized once lesion-level splitting shows how many usable images remain per class.

LEAKAGE PREVENTION — SPLITTING AT THE LESION LEVEL

HAM10000 contains multiple images of the same physical lesion, shot from different angles or zoom levels. Splitting at the image level lets lesion-specific visual cues leak from training into test, inflating apparent performance. MediShield splits at lesion_id instead — the same lesion never appears in more than one of train, validation, or test.

05 — RELIABILITY DETECTOR

XGBoost never diagnoses. It grades the diagnosis.

Labels come from comparing MedicalNet's prediction against ground truth — never from an arbitrary confidence cutoff, which would just make the detector reproduce a threshold instead of learning a genuine signal.

Probabilities
P(bkl), P(mel), P(nv) — the raw class distribution the classifier produced.
Confidence
The maximum class probability — the number a conventional pipeline would stop at.
Prediction margin
The gap between the top-1 and top-2 class probabilities. Melanoma 0.94 vs. nevus 0.04 gives a margin of 0.90 — a decisive call, not a close one.
Entropy
How spread out the distribution is. [0.94, 0.04, 0.02] is far more decisive than [0.36, 0.34, 0.30], even when both share the same top-1 class.
Perturbation behavior
Confidence change, probability shift, whether the predicted class flipped, deep-feature shift, and prediction consistency across perturbations — captured whenever an attack or stress transform is applied.
LABEL DEFINITION

Ground-truth label compared against the MedicalNet prediction: correct → reliable (1), incorrect → unreliable (0). XGBoost then learns the mapping from model behavior to prediction reliability directly, rather than inheriting a rule someone hand-picked.

06 — ADVERSARIAL MACHINE LEARNING

Testing what the classifier does under deliberate pressure.

FGSM and PGD are attack methods, not separate trained models — transformations applied at evaluation time to probe whether a prediction that looked solid actually holds up.

FGSM

FAST GRADIENT SIGN METHOD
Clean image Gradient-based perturbation Adversarial image Classifier

Single-step attack. Measures clean vs. adversarial accuracy, prediction changes, confidence changes, and attack success rate.

PGD

PROJECTED GRADIENT DESCENT
Clean image → small perturbation Model gradient → update Project perturbation → repeat Adversarial image

A stronger, iterative version of FGSM — repeated small steps rather than one large one, giving a tougher stress test.

Adversarial attacks alone aren't representative of everyday failure — so every condition below is also run through the same pipeline, measuring accuracy, confidence, prediction change, reliability score, and abstention rate.

07 — EXPLAINABLE AI

Grad-CAM shows where the model looked — clean vs. perturbed.

Grad-CAM isn't another model — it's a visualization layered on the trained CNN, highlighting which image regions influenced the prediction. MediShield compares the clean-image heatmap against the perturbed-image heatmap; a significant shift in highlighted regions is presented as evidence of explanation instability. It is never claimed to prove medical correctness on its own.

11 — SCIENTIFIC LIMITATIONS, STATED PLAINLY

This is a research prototype. It is not a diagnostic system.

  • HAM10000 is a benchmark dataset, not a complete representation of real-world clinical populations.
  • The three-class setup (bkl, mel, nv) is a narrowed research scope, not full dermatological coverage.
  • FGSM and PGD are controlled experiments; they do not simulate every real-world failure mode.
  • Reliability detection based on prediction correctness does not prove clinical safety.
  • Grad-CAM highlights what the CNN attended to — not necessarily clinically meaningful features.
  • The system is not a replacement for a dermatologist or a clinical diagnostic workflow.

FINAL PROJECT DEFINITION

MediShield challenges the assumption that a confident medical AI prediction is automatically trustworthy. We stress-test the model, learn its reliability patterns, detect suspicious predictions, and allow the system to abstain when confidence alone is not enough.

View the repository Implementation guide

MediShield is a research prototype exploring reliability and safety layers for medical vision models. It should never be presented, marketed, or used as a replacement for a dermatologist or a clinical diagnostic workflow.
